About Kahlil Gibran

Who was Kahlil Gibran?

Kahlil Gibran (1883–1931) was a Lebanese-American poet, writer, philosopher, and visual artist. Born in Bsharri in the Ottoman Empire (modern-day Lebanon), he emigrated to the United States as a child. He is best known for his 1923 book The Prophet, a collection of poetic essays that has been translated into over 100 languages and has never been out of print. Gibran is the third best-selling poet of all time, behind Shakespeare and Lao Tzu.

What is Kahlil Gibran's most famous quote?

Kahlil Gibran's most famous quote is arguably "Your children are not your children. They are the sons and daughters of Life's longing for itself." from his masterpiece The Prophet. Other widely quoted lines include "Out of suffering have emerged the strongest souls" and "Beauty is not in the face; beauty is a light in the heart."

Was Kahlil Gibran married?

No, Kahlil Gibran never married. However, he had two significant relationships that deeply influenced his writing. He maintained an intimate friendship and correspondence with May Ziadeh, a Lebanese-Palestinian writer, for nearly twenty years — though they never met in person. He also had a close relationship with Mary Haskell, an American educator who supported his art and writing throughout his life. Many of Gibran's most powerful words on love, longing, and freedom were shaped by these two connections.

What did Kahlil Gibran say about love?

Love is one of Gibran's most explored themes, especially in The Prophet. He saw love as a force that gives freedom rather than possession: "Love one another, but make not a bond of love: let it rather be a moving sea between the shores of your souls." He also wrote "Love possesses not nor would it be possessed, for love is sufficient unto love" and "Love knows not its own depth until the hour of separation." His writings present love as both liberating and transformative.

How did Kahlil Gibran die?

Kahlil Gibran died on April 10, 1931, in New York City at the age of 48. The cause of death was cirrhosis of the liver and tuberculosis. His body was returned to his hometown of Bsharri, Lebanon, where he was buried in the Mar Sarkis Monastery, which now serves as the Gibran Museum.
