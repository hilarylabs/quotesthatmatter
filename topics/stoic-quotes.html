About Stoicism

What is Stoicism?

Stoicism is an ancient Greek philosophy, later developed in Rome, that teaches virtue, reason, and self-control as the path to a good life. Its central idea is that we can't control outside events, only our own judgments and reactions to them — and that lasting peace of mind comes from focusing our effort there instead.

Who were the most important Stoic philosophers?

The most influential Stoic writers whose work survives today are Marcus Aurelius, a Roman Emperor whose private journal became Meditations; Seneca the Younger, a statesman and playwright known for his letters and essays; and Epictetus, a former slave turned teacher whose lectures were recorded by a student as the Discourses and Enchiridion.

What are the best Stoic quotes for daily life?

Some of the most quoted lines of Stoic wisdom come from Marcus Aurelius's Meditations and Seneca's letters, covering themes like accepting what you can't control, not wasting time, and treating each day as valuable. They're widely used as short, practical reminders for handling stress, setbacks, and everyday decisions.

Is Stoicism still relevant today?

Yes — Stoicism has seen a major resurgence in recent years, particularly through modern writers and the popularity of Meditations as a guide to resilience. Its core ideas about focusing on what's within your control translate directly to modern concerns like work stress, social comparison, and information overload.
