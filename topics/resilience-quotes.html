About Resilience

What is resilience?

Resilience is the ability to adapt, recover, and keep moving forward in the face of adversity, trauma, or significant stress. It doesn't mean avoiding hardship or not being affected by it — it means being able to endure it, adapt to it, and come out the other side without being permanently broken by it.

Who are some famous examples of resilience?

David Goggins overcame poverty, abuse, and severe obesity to become a Navy SEAL and elite endurance athlete. Nelson Mandela endured 27 years in prison before leading South Africa's transition to democracy. Maya Angelou survived a traumatic childhood to become one of the most celebrated writers of the 20th century. Viktor Frankl survived multiple Nazi concentration camps and used that experience to found an entire school of psychotherapy centered on meaning.

What are the best resilience quotes for hard times?

Some of the most quoted lines on resilience include David Goggins on “callusing the mind,” Nelson Mandela on rising every time you fall, Maya Angelou on refusing to be reduced by what happens to you, and Viktor Frankl on choosing your response between stimulus and reaction. They're widely used as short, practical reminders for getting through setbacks, loss, and adversity.

How can you build resilience?

Psychologists generally point to a few common threads: reframing how you interpret setbacks, maintaining a sense of purpose or meaning, leaning on relationships and community rather than isolating, and building through repeated exposure to manageable difficulty rather than avoiding it entirely.
