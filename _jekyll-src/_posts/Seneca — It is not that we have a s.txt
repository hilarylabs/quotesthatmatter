who is seneca?
Lucius Annaeus Seneca the Younger (c. 4 BC – 65 AD) was a Roman Stoic philosopher, statesman, dramatist, and tutor to the Emperor Nero. Born in Cordoba, Hispania, he became one of the most prominent intellectuals of the Roman Empire.

What is Seneca best known for?

Seneca is best known for his Letters to Lucilius (Epistulae Morales), a collection of 124 letters addressed to his friend Lucilius that explore Stoic philosophy in practical, personal terms. He is also celebrated for his essays On the Shortness of Life, On Anger, and On the Happy Life, and for bringing Stoicism to life in plain, memorable prose that still resonates today.

How did Seneca die?

Seneca was ordered to commit suicide in 65 AD by Emperor Nero, following his alleged involvement in the Pisonian conspiracy to assassinate the emperor. According to Tacitus, he faced death with calm dignity — in the Stoic manner he had long advocated — surrounded by friends, dictating his final thoughts before opening his veins.
